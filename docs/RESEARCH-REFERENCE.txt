 01 Executive findings and decisions
 The UAE food-service sector should be modelled as a family of connected operating businesses. A
 table-service restaurant sells a customer experience; a cloud kitchen manages channel demand and
 kitchen capacity; a mess operator fulfils recurring meal entitlements; a central kitchen produces and
 transfers batches. They share ingredients and accounting, but the commercial record and the
 operational proof differ substantially.

 This report recommends a DigitalBurj Restaurant & Food Service OS with three operational engines:
 customer orders, contracted meals, and production batches. Each engine should feed one
 controlled inventory ledger, traceability record and financial reconciliation layer. This is a product design
 recommendation, not a statement that authorities prescribe these software modules.

 What the research establishes
 • Federal food-safety legislation sits alongside local food-establishment approvals and inspection
 processes. The official federal portal identifies Federal Law No. 10 of 2015 as the food-safety framework.
 [S02]

 • Dubai Municipality publishes a food-establishment guidance directory; Abu Dhabi publishes EFST
 information; Sharjah, Ajman, Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah publish different approval or operational
 services. Local settings cannot be reduced to one Dubai checklist. [S03, S06, S08–S15]

 • Dubai DET reported almost 1,200 new restaurant licences during 2024. This is a historical Dubai
 market signal, not the number of active UAE restaurants or a 2026 market-size estimate. [S01]

 • Platform integration requires partner access. Talabat documents integration interfaces; Deliveroo
 distinguishes sandbox access from production access after a signed contract. [S22–S24]

 Recommended initial scope
 Launch one dependable operating core and three packages: Restaurant & Café, Cloud Kitchen, and Mess
 & Contract Meals. Add catering, central production and meal subscriptions after the core records have
 been proven in pilots. Food manufacturing, hospital meals and airport operations should have specialist
 extensions and domain review rather than being presented as ordinary restaurant configurations.

 The most important product promise is operational evidence: what was ordered or contracted, what was
 prepared, which ingredients were used, what was delivered or served, what was charged, and what was
 actually collected. A dashboard is only useful when these records reconcile.

 Immediate decisions
 Approve the common data model; recruit pilots across the three operating engines; verify each pilot's
 actual premises jurisdiction and activities; establish recipe and opening-stock quality; secure written
 platform access; and select an accounting/eInvoicing integration approach. Avoid claiming government
 certification, live municipal APIs or automatic compliance before those arrangements exist.

 02 Scope, research method and evidence
 limits
 This report covers all seven emirates geographically and the principal commercial food-service formats
 functionally. It is a desk-researched operating and product blueprint. It does not claim a census of
 establishments, primary interviews with operators, an exhaustive legal opinion, or validated vendor
 pricing. No unsupported UAE-wide establishment count, market value or market-share ranking is used.

 Four labels used throughout

   Label                             Meaning

   Verified evidence                 A specific published official or first-party source supports the nearby factual
                                     statement. Source IDs lead to the reference register.

   Design recommendation             A proposed DigitalBurj workflow, control, field, metric or architecture. It requires
                                     validation with actual operators.

   Illustration                      Invented numbers used to demonstrate calculation or process. They are not UAE
                                     benchmarks or supplier quotations.

   Verification gap                  A detail not established by the reviewed evidence. Confirm with the relevant
                                     authority, contract, professional or provider before implementation.



 Research approach
 Priority was given to UAE federal portals, the Federal Tax Authority, Ministry of Finance, municipal and
 food-authority pages, DET publications and delivery-platform developer documentation. Older manuals
 remain useful design references but must be checked for amendments and applicability. Several service
 pages were available through indexed official summaries when the full page did not render; the register
 identifies these limitations.

 The reference register distinguishes sources directly supporting findings from supporting guidance.
 Product recommendations are original synthesis and are not attributed to an authority merely because a
 source appears in the same chapter. Exact temperature limits, shelf lives, document retention, permit
 fees and processing times are intentionally left configurable unless verified for the actual activity and
 jurisdiction.

 Material gaps
 The reviewed public sources did not establish a complete food-activity approval checklist for Umm Al
 Quwain. Sharjah city and Fujairah city service pages should not be assumed to govern every
 municipality within those emirates. Free zones, industrial premises, ports, airport locations and event
 venues require a site-specific authority map. Prices, commissions, platform availability and permitted
 kitchen sharing depend on commercial contracts and approvals.

 Research cutoff is 11 October 2026. Regulatory and integration findings should be rechecked at
 onboarding and on a scheduled basis. Each customer should own a dated compliance register recording
 the authority, legal entity, approved activity, premises, source document, expiry, responsible person
 and last verification date.

 04 Market context and opportunity
 assessment
 The strongest usable market evidence in this research is a dated Dubai signal: DET's 2024 gastronomy
 publication, announced in April 2025, reported almost 1,200 new restaurant licences in 2024. It supports
 the existence of active new-business formation in Dubai. It does not establish active establishment
 totals, closure rates, profitability or conditions in the other six emirates. [S01]

 The software opportunity should therefore be sized from a verified customer pipeline, not by multiplying
 an assumed national restaurant count by a hypothetical subscription fee. Build a bottom-up register of
 reachable operators, their locations, operating models, current systems, switching constraints and
 willingness to pay.

 Priority hypotheses to validate
 Restaurant and café owners may value a reliable close-of-day view, recipe cost and direct ordering.
 Cloud-kitchen managers may value consolidated tickets, menu availability and platform settlement
 reconciliation. Mess operators may value headcount cutoffs, contract-rate versioning, meal acceptance
 and invoice evidence. These are testable product hypotheses, not surveyed conclusions.

 Interview operators in Dubai and Abu Dhabi, industrial and residential catchments in Sharjah and Ajman,
 and at least one operator each in RAK, Fujairah and UAQ. Include single sites and small chains. Recruit
 Arabic- and English-speaking decision makers and the actual shift staff. Capture seasonal peaks,
 Ramadan arrangements, weekends, shift-based meal patterns and emergency operating procedures
 without assuming they affect every business equally.

 No recommendation depends on an unsupported claim that one emirate is cheaper, easier to license or
 more profitable. Compare premises and operating economics using quotations and approved activities
 for the specific site.

 05 UAE licensing and regulatory control
 structure
 Federal food-safety legislation establishes the national framework, while premises approval and
 operational administration require local authority verification. The official UAE portal identifies Federal
 Law No. 10 of 2015 on food safety. [S02] The correct implementation is a register of applicable
 obligations, not a single badge labelled “UAE compliant.”

 Recommended approval sequence
 1. Identify the legal entity, licensing authority, exact activity and premises jurisdiction. 2. Confirm
 whether the intended kitchen, shared facility, mobile operation or production activity is permitted at
 that site. 3. Obtain the applicable preliminary approvals and assess the layout before committing to
 fit-out. 4. Coordinate landlord, building, engineering, sanitation, fire and other approvals where
 applicable. 5. Complete the food-authority inspection and operational permissions relevant to the
 establishment. 6. Verify food-handler training/health requirements, transport permissions, product
 registrations and special-event arrangements. 7. Record the approved documents, renewals and
 limitations; reassess changes to activity, layout, ownership or delivery arrangements.

 This sequence is a practical project control. The actual order and required documents must follow the
 responsible authority's service procedure. Do not treat a trade licence alone as proof that all food
 operations, vehicle uses or off-site events are authorised.

 Special site and activity checks
 Dubai Development Authority publishes a catering service permit process for its relevant sites, including
 specified supporting approvals and documents. This is a jurisdiction-specific example, not a permit
 required for all UAE catering. [S07] A mall, free-zone facility, hotel, industrial unit or event venue may
 introduce additional approval relationships that should be mapped during onboarding.

 Dubai's calorie-declaration guidance is a reference for nutrition disclosure; its date and applicability
 must be checked before making a menu-labeling claim. A recipe software calculation alone should not
 be presented as an approved laboratory determination. [S27]

 For safe surplus redistribution, Dubai Municipality's Food Bank material demonstrates an organised
 channel. A product should support authorised collection records and acceptance decisions, not
 automatically classify expired or uncontrolled food as suitable for donation. [S28]

 No Dubai fee schedule or processing-time estimate is assumed in the commercial plan. Obtain current
 quotations and the authority's actual service conditions for the selected activity.

 07 Abu Dhabi: food handlers and safety
 management
 ADAFSA's food-safety page describes the Essential Food Safety Training programme for food handlers
 and covers core handling topics. Its EFST FAQ material provides additional programme information.
 These are the principal researched training references for Abu Dhabi. [S06, S26]

 ADAFSA's Salamt Zadna material describes a practical food-safety management approach for small
 independent catering businesses, including procedures and diary-style records. It can inform the
 structure of operational checklists, subject to confirming current applicability and obtaining the
 operator's approved procedures. [S29]

 Recommended Abu Dhabi operating record
 • Establishment and legal-entity identifiers, approved activities, facility location and actual authority
 contact.

 • Food-handler roster linked to role, training evidence and any applicable health documentation, with
 restricted access to personal records.

 • Shift assignment of responsibility for cooking, chilling, cleaning and cross-contamination controls.

 • A dated diary of checks, deviations, corrective actions, verification and responsible-person sign-off.

 • Supplier, receipt, lot and batch records that connect incoming ingredients to meals and destinations.

 Institutional and multi-site operations
 For Al Ain, Al Dhafra and Abu Dhabi city, identify the precise site and relevant authority relationship
 rather than assuming a single office process or inspection condition. A central kitchen supplying several
 sites needs receiving confirmation at each destination and visibility of food held in transit. A production
 record marked “complete” is insufficient evidence that the meal arrived and was accepted.

 Schools, hospitals, care institutions and worker accommodation may impose contract-specific meal,
 nutrition, service-time or incident requirements. Capture those as versioned contract schedules; a
 general restaurant checklist should not overwrite them. Patient-specific dietary instructions require
 authorised clinical review and stronger privacy controls.

 Design recommendation: generate an inspection evidence pack containing the establishment document
 index, trained-staff register, selected check logs, incident history and batch-trace report. The export
 should state its date range and missing items. Avoid calling it an ADAFSA-approved certificate unless
 formally authorised.

 Verification before launch should include actual training status, premises permissions, approved
 procedures, transport arrangements, supplier/product requirements and contract obligations.
 EFST-related requirements should not be copied verbatim into other emirates as though the programme
 were national.

 Practical implications for small operators
 A cafeteria using manual notebooks may not have an organised document archive. Start by
 photographing and indexing the actual documents, checking readable dates and identifying the legal
 entity that holds them. Do not prefill “approved” simply because the shop is trading. Resolve document
 ownership when the kitchen is rented, managed by a third party or operated under several brands.

 For cross-emirate deliveries, capture origin facility and destination independently. A kitchen licensed in
 one emirate does not establish that every delivery vehicle, satellite serving point or packaged product
 meets all destination conditions. The application should expose the unanswered permission questions
 rather than invent a universal cross-emirate permit.

 Product configuration should include Arabic and English names, local municipality, activity description,
 plan version, document type, issuing body, renewal lead time and evidence owner. This makes the same
 software adaptable while preserving the actual local process.

 Product handling of incomplete regulatory evidence
 Keep a task status of “requires authority confirmation,” with the question, owner, contact date and
 supporting reply. Allow a document to be uploaded without automatically marking the underlying
 obligation satisfied. Preserve the distinction between evidence received, evidence reviewed and
 authority approval obtained.

 For all three emirates, build one jurisdiction record per facility and serving site. Record home-based,
 industrial, mobile, event, hotel, shared-kitchen and packaged-product activities individually. Site
 permissions and accepted operating procedures should govern configurable checks.

 Geographic coverage in this report means each emirate has an explicit treatment and verification route.
 It does not mean every local rule has been researched to the same depth or that unpublished approval
 conditions have been validated. The UAQ gap should be closed before a UAQ pilot is represented as
 regulatory-ready.

 Common order lifecycle
 Create → validate menu and modifiers → accept → route to station → prepare → quality/packing check →
 serve or dispatch → settle → close. Cancellation can occur at different stages and must record whether
 production had begun. A refund is a financial event; ingredient wastage is an operational event. They
 may be linked but must not be silently equated.

 Design recommendation: allow unavailable items to be blocked by branch and channel. Prevent a
 modifier that has no recipe consequence from making cost reports appear complete. Where stock
 accuracy is weak, show availability as manager-controlled rather than pretending the system can
 promise live quantities.

 The daily close should compare bills, cash, card totals, refunds, discounts, house accounts and
 delivery-channel orders. Unsettled tables, payment exceptions and pending platform payouts should
 remain visible rather than disappearing at midnight. Store both the service date and the actual
 timestamp, using Asia/Dubai local time for operational reports.

 11 Cloud kitchens: brands, facilities and
 channel operations
 A cloud kitchen is a production and delivery operation. Distinguish an operator running its own brands, a
 virtual brand produced by an existing restaurant, and a shared facility hosting independent businesses.
 Facility ownership, food responsibility, menu ownership and channel contracts can belong to different
 parties.

   Model                      Required commercial structure              Operational distinction

   Single brand               One brand, one or more channels,           Simple station routing, packaging and pickup
                              identified legal operator                  timing.

   Multi-brand operator       Brands linked to one operator and shared   Shared ingredients/capacity; separate
                              facilities                                 channel IDs, menus and profitability.

   Virtual brand              Host production agreement and              Trace each order to host facility, recipe and
                              brand/channel ownership                    settlement beneficiary.

   Shared kitchen facility    Tenant contracts, space/equipment          Strong tenant data isolation; shared services
                              rights and responsibility map              billing; no automatic pooled stock.



 Channel and menu control
 Maintain a canonical item and recipe, then map each channel's item IDs, descriptions, photographs,
 modifier rules, availability and prices. Preserve a published-menu version. A menu update is not
 complete until the channel acknowledges it, and partial failure must show which outlet or item failed.

 Talabat's first-party documentation covers menu, vendor and order integration concepts. Deliveroo
 publishes partner developer interfaces and states that production access follows commercial
 contracting. These sources support a partner-access strategy, not an assumption that every merchant
 can immediately use every endpoint. [S22–S24]

 Capacity and dispatch
 Calculate workload by preparation station and promised collection window. Two brands selling the same
 fried product consume the same fryer capacity; the system must not independently promise unlimited
 capacity for each brand. Show a manager the options: adjust preparation time, pause selected items,
 limit orders or assign additional capacity.

 Use a packing checklist keyed to the channel order number, including modifiers, sauce, cutlery policy,
 beverages and sealed-bag identifiers where adopted. Record courier arrival separately from kitchen
 readiness and actual handover. This distinguishes late production from waiting for a rider.

 Operational permission to host multiple brands, share a kitchen or rent stations must be verified for the
 actual facility. A software brand record does not create a legal right to operate that brand at that
 kitchen.

 22 Recipes, yield, portions and costing
 A recipe is a versioned production specification. It must include inputs, units, output yield, portions,
 modifiers, components, waste assumptions and approved preparation information. Cuisine labels alone
 are insufficient to calculate cost or trace an allergen.

 Illustrative recipe conversion
 An invented batch uses 10 kg of raw ingredient and yields 8 kg of usable cooked component. Yield is
 80%. If raw cost is AED 10/kg, input cost is AED 100 and usable output cost is AED 12.50/kg before other
 ingredients, labour or overhead. A 200 g portion consumes AED 2.50 of that component. This is a
 calculation example, not a UAE food-price benchmark.

   Recipe layer                      Purpose

   Ingredient master                 Units, supplier mapping, lot/expiry treatment, allergen metadata and approved
                                     substitutions.

   Component recipe                  Sauce, cooked rice, dough or marinated item produced as a batch.

   Finished recipe                   Actual menu item or meal with component quantities and portion yield.

   Modifier rule                     Add/remove/swap ingredient or component, with quantity and price consequences.

   Packaging recipe                  Box, cup, lid, bag, label and optional accessories by channel.

   Version approval                  Effective time, author, reviewer, reason and affected menus/plans.



 Cost methods
 Show a current estimated recipe cost and a historical transaction cost separately. Define whether the
 stock valuation method is weighted average, FIFO or another accountant-approved method. Keep cost
 assumptions visible, and avoid silently recalculating last month's profit from today's ingredient prices.

 Theoretical consumption equals fulfilled quantities multiplied by the applicable recipe version. Actual
 consumption comes from stock movements and counts. Their difference may reflect yield, waste,
 unrecorded meals, incorrect recipes, counting errors or theft; the software should identify variance
 without automatically assigning blame.

 Bulk meals and buffets need recipe-based planned output and actual batch yield. They cannot rely
 solely on point-of-sale items to describe replenishment and leftovers. Central kitchens need component
 cost transfer to outlets so the same raw ingredients are not expensed twice.

 Allergen inheritance should flow from ingredient to component to finished recipe and modifier.
 Conflicting or missing metadata should block an unsupported “allergen-free” declaration. Staff
 communication, cross-contact procedures and authorised review remain necessary even when recipe
 metadata appears complete.

 Traceability test

 A broad exposure window is preferable to fabricated precision.

 24 Food safety, allergens and corrective

 The food-safety module should digitise the operator's applicable procedures and evidence. It should not
 invent universal control limits or claim certification. Official Dubai guidance, Abu Dhabi training material
 and RAK requirements provide researched local anchors, but applicability and current versions require

 Recommended record design
 Each check has a facility, location/equipment, procedure version, required frequency, authorised limit or
 criterion, measurement unit, time, staff member and result. A failed check creates a deviation with
 immediate action, affected stock/batches, escalation, verification and closure approval. Editing a
 reading requires an auditable correction.

   Control area                      Example evidence structure

   Receipt and storage               Accepted condition, lot, relevant measurements, segregation and stock hold.

   Preparation/cooking               Batch and procedure, instrument identity, recorded readings and exceptions.

   Cooling/holding/reheating         Applicable approved procedure and timed evidence; do not assume one threshold
                                     for every product.

   Cleaning/sanitation               Area/equipment, task, chemical/procedure version, completion and verification.

   Cross-contamination/allergens     Ingredient metadata, station/utensil controls, substitution review and staff
                                     communication.

   Pest/equipment control            Contractor visit, findings, repair/maintenance and corrective action.

   Staff readiness                   Applicable training/health evidence, task assignment and restricted personnel
                                     access.



 Alert design
 Escalate missed or failed checks according to the approved policy. Keep alerts actionable: what failed,
 affected product/equipment, required owner and next step. Repeated notifications with no owner create
 the illusion of control. A notification read is not corrective-action completion.

 Do not allow AI to release quarantined stock, approve unsafe substitutions, manufacture a missing
 temperature reading or close a safety incident. An assistant may summarise trends, locate a procedure
 and draft a corrective-action report for review. Missing data must remain missing.

 Calibration and measurement quality matter. Store instrument identity and maintenance/calibration
 evidence where used. If manual entry occurs later, show event and entry times separately. Photos
 supplement records but are not proof that a measurement was performed correctly.

 The food-safety responsible person owns the procedure and final decision. Software completion
 statistics should never be marketed as a regulator's inspection outcome.

 27 Finance, UAE tax and eInvoicing
 readiness
 Finance needs a controlled flow from operational records to sales, receivables, payables, stock cost and

 FTA's VAT-registration page gives the resident-business mandatory threshold as AED 375,000 and
 voluntary threshold as AED 187,500, with the relevant backward/forward tests; different rules can apply

 Thresholds are not a substitute for transaction-level tax analysis.

 The general corporate-tax guide describes the standard regime; applicable reliefs, exclusions, free-zone
 treatment and special regimes need adviser review. The AED 375,000 taxable-income threshold under
 the general corporate-tax rate structure is conceptually different from the VAT registration turnover

 State machines — recommendation
 Order: draft → validated → accepted → in preparation → ready → fulfilled → closed; cancellation/refund is
 a controlled parallel path with stage evidence. Batch: planned → released for production → executing →
 hold/review → released or rejected → allocated/consumed. Dispatch: planned → loaded → in transit →

 changes. Quality hold cannot be cleared by an ordinary cashier role.

 insufficient to reconcile an external order or invoice. Record incomplete evidence honestly and prevent
 dashboards from treating missing fields as zero.

 31 Privacy, security, offline operation and
 resilience
 The official UAE portal identifies the federal personal-data protection framework, including Federal
 Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021. Applicability, exceptions and sector/free-zone regimes require assessment
 for the operator. Do not assume blanket UAE-only hosting for all information or blanket permission for
 international transfer. [S25]

 Design recommendations
 • Separate tenants and legal entities; test that users cannot query another operator's contracts, worker
 roster or financial records.

 • Use role-based access, MFA for privileged accounts, short-lived sessions where appropriate and an
 audited emergency-access procedure.

 • Encrypt transport and stored sensitive data; protect backup access and secret rotation.

 • Minimise customer and worker data; restrict dietary/health details and identity-document access.

 • Maintain configurable retention, lawful-access handling, notices and approved deletion/archiving
 procedures.

 Offline workflows
 Allow local order capture and printing with durable IDs. Display pending-sync state. Define which offline
 actions are permitted: basic sales or meal serving may be allowed; tax configuration, issued-invoice
 edits, recipe publication or cross-site stock promises should require stronger coordination. After
 reconnection, reconcile duplicates, version conflicts and payment status without charging twice.

 For mess serving points, use a signed roster/entitlement snapshot and record exceptions. Do not silently
 grant or remove entitlement on stale data. For card payments, follow the payment provider's actual
 offline capability; the OS must not falsely display an unpaid or unknown authorisation as paid.

 Service resilience
 Define measurable backup and recovery targets with the customer; test restoration, not only backup
 creation. Plan internet loss, power/device failure, platform outage, printer failure, stock data corruption
 and staff unavailability. Keep the essential paper/manual operating procedure available for food checks
 and service.

 Monitor queues, sync lag, failed integrations, rejected finance exports and aged exceptions. An
 administrator should have an incident runbook, customer contact route and reconciliation procedure.
 Recovery requires confirming records, not simply restarting the server.

 Cloud hosting and vendor contracts should be assessed against the actual data, regulatory and
 customer requirements. Preserve exportability so an SME can leave the product with usable operational
 and financial records.

 Recommended pricing dimensions
 Base entity/facility package; number of outlets; production sites; mess sites/active contracts; channel
 connectors; device or serving-point needs; specialist extensions; onboarding/data-cleaning effort;
 support level. Separate third-party ASP, payment, platform, hardware and messaging charges so
 customers understand actual total cost.

 Avoid making every kitchen worker a paid administrative seat if that makes essential safety tasks
 unattractive to use. Consider task-limited users and clear fair-use rules. Do not bill worker meals twice
 merely because both roster and scan counts exist. Align the pricing unit with the service being provided
 by the software.

 Discovery questionnaire
 • What are the approved activities, legal entities, premises and serving/delivery locations?

 • Who orders, who consumes, who approves and who pays? What evidence determines the invoice?

 • Which formats and channels operate together, and where is stock owned or shared?

 • Are recipes, portions, yields, pack conversions and opening stock reliable?

 • What is the current daily close or month-end meal reconciliation process?

 • Which exceptions recur: refunds, wasted production, missed meals, disputed counts, delayed
 collections?

 • Which food checks, training, vehicle and product documents are actually required at the site?

 • What devices, internet conditions, accounting system and platform contracts exist?

 • What information is sensitive, who can access it and what must be retained/exported?

 • What must work on the first day, and which measurable result justifies payment?

 Onboarding deliverables
 A customer-specific workflow map; module/package selection; evidence register; migration workbook;
 tested recipe and contract examples; permission matrix; training plan; signed acceptance criteria;
 support/fallback guide; financial and stock opening reconciliation.

 Estimate onboarding after inspecting the actual data. A restaurant with 50 well-maintained recipes
 differs from a mess operator with changing rosters and undocumented billing rules. Charge or scope
 data cleaning explicitly rather than hiding it inside a low subscription and delivering incomplete cost
 reports.

 Retention should depend on reliable daily use and explainable business value. Provide usable exports of
 menus, recipes, stock movements, contracts and financial records so customers retain control of their
 operations.
